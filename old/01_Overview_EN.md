# Sai 사이 — Project Overview

**Furniture — the smallest unit that connects people and space**
Jin Choi · IDAS, Hongik University · Digital Media Design · 2026

---

## 0. In one sentence

> In a country where 36.1% of all households are one person living alone, the room does nothing when that person walks in.
> This project redefines **furniture as the smallest unit connecting a person to a space**, proves it with two works, and grows it into a total space design practice.

---

## 1. The problem

### 1-1. The starting point — the best housing, the loneliest people

Korea ranks **first of 38 countries** on the housing dimension of the OECD Better Life Index.
In the same index, on *"do you have someone to rely on in a crisis"*, Korea ranks **38th of 38 — last.**
Life satisfaction ranks 36th (6.5 points); the composite ranking is 19th (5.99).

> We live in the best-built homes on earth and are the loneliest people inside them.

Housing is graded on floor area, structural safety and price. **Whether a space acknowledges
the person inside it** is on nobody's list.

### 1-6. Problem statement

> **Loneliness is a matter between people. But the place where those hours are spent is the room.**

**The house got better. The room did not.**
Korea grades a home on three things: how many square metres, how safe, how much it costs.
Nothing on that list asks what the space actually does for the person inside it.

## 2. The position

### 2-1. Architecture is too slow. Furniture is already in the room.

**① The smallest architecture**
Furniture is the smallest act that defines how a body relates to a space.
Change the furniture and you change the relationship — without touching the building.

**② The only architecture a tenant owns**
82.6% of young households cannot modify a wall, a window or a doorway. The furniture is theirs.

**③ The contact already exists**
People already hang coats on furniture, sit on it, pass through it every day.
The furniture simply produces no response — and a response can be designed.

> **The intervention is not the building. It is the furniture standing in it.**

### 2-2. Hypothesis

> **If furniture responds to a person, the space begins to acknowledge that person.**

| | Mechanism | Description |
|---|---|---|
| 01 | **Arrival** | Something changes state for no reason other than that I came in |
| 02 | **Presence** | The space remembers how long I stayed |

### 2-3. Material — cold by intent

Steel, acrylic, aluminium. Not one warm material in either work — no wood, no fabric.

**Why.** Wood or fabric would have made the furniture look warm from the start.
Then any warmth produced when the light comes on could not be told apart from the warmth
of the material itself.

> **Warmth does not come from the material. It is evidence that a person was here.**

| Decision | Reason |
|---|---|
| **Cold materials only** | The baseline must be fully cold for the cause of any warmth to be legible |
| **Achromatic surfaces** | Steel and clear acrylic have no colour temperature of their own |
| **Warm light (~2700K)** | The only warm element in either work — and a person always causes it |
| **No soft finish** | Nothing looks comfortable until a body switches the object on |

*(cuttable in the talk)* These are also the real materials of a Korean rented room — aluminium sash,
acrylic, painted steel. Rather than replacing them, the work adds one condition to them.

### 2-4. Why digital media ⚠️ *key defence point*

| | Argument | Description |
|---|---|---|
| **01** | **Time becomes a material** | To hold light for exactly as long as someone sat, the furniture has to know a person sat down, measure that duration, store it and play it back. An inert object cannot hold time. **Computation is what makes time a material.** |
| **02** | **The furniture becomes the interface** | No button, no app, no screen. A switch has to be pressed deliberately; **load sensing simply reads an action you were already doing.** There is nothing to learn, and that is the point. |
| **03** | **Causality becomes evidence** | The counter displays the calculation itself. The moment the number is visible, this stops being a nice light and becomes **a relationship between me and the space.** |
| **04** | **The work becomes an instrument** | A wooden stool leaves no record of who sat on it or for how long. **This one does** — which makes the artwork and the research apparatus the same object. |

> **Digital media here is not a medium of expression. It is the minimum condition for treating time as a material.**

**The sensing choice is the argument.** Load and occupancy sensing were chosen over switches and
motion detectors because they read the weight of something actually entrusted to the furniture,
not an intention to operate it.

## 3. The system — two works, one route

```
        00 Outside               01 Entrance          02 Dwelling
  light · weather · sound   →   Warm Welcome    →      Yellow Box
  · other people
```

The two works take the two moments between coming home and leaving again:
stepping through the door, and the time you spend sitting.

| | **Warm Welcome** | **Yellow Box** |
|---|---|---|
| Korean title | 따뜻한 환영 | 옐로우 박스 |
| Position | Entrance | Dwelling |
| Relation | Person ↔ Space | Person ↔ Time ↔ Space |
| Trigger | Weight of a coat or bag | Body weight on the seat |
| Response | Backlight on, sustained | Light on; afterglow equal to dwell time |
| What it proves | The space recognises whoever came back | Time spent leaves a trace |

## 4. The works

### 4-1. Warm Welcome — Entrance

**Build:** steel frame, frosted acrylic, backlight, load sensing. 1800 mm tall.

A coat rack you keep near the entrance. You come home and hang the bag and jacket you were
already carrying. That weight closes a circuit and light comes on from behind.
**You leave the public version of yourself hanging here, and step into your own space.**

- **A coat rack as tall as a person** is not something you hang a coat on; it becomes the door into your own space.
- The hanging garment becomes a silhouette — the shape of your own body, standing in light.

### 4-2. Yellow Box — Dwelling

**Build:** frosted acrylic on three faces, internal light, seat occupancy sensing, rear counter.

Sit and it lights. Stand and it does not switch off.
**It stays lit for exactly as long as you sat, then fades.** Ten minutes of sitting leaves ten minutes.

- A room is finished by whoever was last inside it — and it stays finished for a while after they go.
- The afterglow is not a delay but a **trace**, proportional to exactly how long a person stayed.
- In architecture the chair defines the smallest relationship between body and space. This one records it.

**The rear counter**
It counts up while you sit and counts down once you leave.
If the light on the front is the feeling, the number on the back is the reason it is there.
Without the number it reads as a mood lamp, or a light that takes a while to switch off.
With the number you can recognise that this light exists because of the time you spent sitting.

> **And the number does one more thing in an exhibition:
> every visitor who sits down leaves one dwell-time record behind. No questionnaire, no prompting.**

### 4-3. Technical stack

> ⚠️ In the presentation, describe this at the level of **sensing method**. Give specific components verbally only if asked.

| Work | Senses | Processes | Responds |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Warm Welcome** | Load at the hanging bar | Threshold detection, state held while loaded | Backlight on, sustained |
| **Yellow Box** | Seat occupancy | Duration measured, stored, replayed 1:1, then faded | Dimmed light + numeric display + dwell log |

**Why sense load.** A switch or a motion detector would have worked. Load was chosen because it
reads the weight of something actually entrusted to the furniture, not an intention to operate it.
Hanging a coat and sitting down become the signal as they are.

**Why no screen.** A smart home asks you to install an app and look at a display.
This furniture asks for nothing. **It only gets used daily if there is nothing to learn.**

## 5. Limits

This must be defended in the exam, and it is stronger presented first.

### What this does **not** claim
- A stool does not solve loneliness or isolation. Those are serious social problems, and furniture does not reach that far.
- These works are **a hypothesis about how people perceive space**, not welfare policy.
- No physiological or long-term effect was measured.

### What it does claim
- **Furniture can respond to a person, and that response is something you design.**
- You decide what it responds to, how it responds and how long it lasts — which also means you can check whether it works.
- **You cannot change the building, but you can change the furniture.** It reaches into the room at a cost and scale architecture cannot.

## 6. DDP, November 2026

The two works are installed in the order entrance · dwelling, so a visitor physically walks
the route this presentation describes.

> **In the deck this is a single slide reading "DDP Seoul / November 2026".
> What follows is material for the thesis and for Q&A.**

| Instrument | Yields |
|---|---|
| **Automatic dwell log** (Yellow Box counter) | Seating duration per visitor, continuous and unprompted |
| **Exit survey** (KR/EN, 12 items, ~3 min) | Comprehension, warmth vs. surveillance, willingness to live with it |
| **Observation** (timed sampling) | Approach rate, hesitation, repeat interaction, solo vs. group |
| **Documentation** | Thesis plates and portfolio record |

### What the exhibition is there to find out

| | Question | Design |
|---|---|---|
| 01 | **Is it read?** | Without reading any text, does a visitor realise the light came on because of them? |
| 02 | **Is it felt?** | Does being recognised by furniture feel welcoming, or like being watched? |
| 03 | **Does the counter help?** | Compare comprehension between visitors who saw the rear digits and those who did not |
| 04 | **Would they live with it?** | What is good in an exhibition and what you want at home are different |

> **Only question 3 splits into comparison groups naturally.
> Whether people understand the work better when the number is visible will be the thesis's central result.**

## 7. Roadmap

| Period | Stage | Description |
|---|---|---|
| 2025 — 2026 | **Object** | Two works, two relations, one rule. Shown at DDP as a single route |
| 2027 | **Set** | Furniture responds to furniture. The hanger knows whether the stool is occupied |
| 2028 | **Room** | A whole one-person dwelling designed as a responsive space, piloted with housing operators |
| 2029 — | **Space** | Expansion into total space design: hotels, retail, public interiors |

> One principle runs from beginning to end. **A space should respond to the person inside it.**
> A stool or a building — it makes no difference.
